I am an applied economist and statistician at **Lancaster University**. My research sits at the intersection of **international macroeconomics, technology diffusion and productivity**, using applied econometric and time-series methods to study how innovation spreads across countries and sectors, and what it means for productivity, labour markets and policy.

I currently work as a Statistician on the UKRI-funded project *A Multi-Dimensional Understanding of the Digital Far Right*, based in the Department of Linguistics and English Language, where I develop and evaluate statistical methods for zero-inflated and sparse multivariate data. I hold a PhD in Economics from Lancaster University.

My work has been presented to the UK **Department for Business and Trade** and the **National Institute of Economic and Social Research (NIESR)**, as well as at conferences including the IEA World Congress, CEF and SNDE. I have eight years of teaching experience and am an Associate Fellow of Advance HE (AFHEA).

## Research interests

International macroeconomics · Technology diffusion and innovation policy · Productivity · Applied econometrics

## Current research

**[Pre-Grant Patents and Innovation Diffusion](/research/)**<br>
Uses pre-grant patent flows to show that international technology spillovers run mainly through the global stock of knowledge rather than trade, raising TFP and R&amp;D capital.

**[Global Technology Shocks and Labor Dynamics: Aggregate and Sectoral Evidence from OECD Countries](/research/)**<br>
Traces how global technology shocks diffuse through traded sectors across 18 OECD countries, and how borrowing capacity and labour mobility shape the gains.
