*UKRI-funded project · Department of Linguistics and English Language, Lancaster University*

## About the project

*A Multi-Dimensional Understanding of the Digital Far Right* is an interdisciplinary project led by Dr Isobelle Clarke at Lancaster University. It brings together corpus-assisted discourse analysis, forensic linguistics and cybersecurity to study how far-right groups communicate and organise online. By comparing linguistic repertoires and patterns of online activity across groups, the project maps variation within far-right extremism and develops evidence-based indicators to support offender prioritisation and inform law-enforcement training.

## My role

I am the project's Research Associate – Statistician. My work focuses on the statistical side of the project:

* Developing and evaluating **zero-inflated models** and **multivariate statistical methods** for sparse text and language data, where many features occur rarely or not at all in individual texts.
* Contributing to **statistical software package development** that makes these methods available to the wider research community.
* Supporting **team training** in quantitative methods, and contributing to **manuscripts and research outputs**.
